How it works

Three steps. No site visit. No hourly meter running.

Every job follows the same documented pipeline — the same one used to produce the sample package below. Your intake form drops directly into step one.

STEP 1 — YOU SEND

Program, drawings, photos

Your PLC export (.ACD / L5X, TIA Portal, or a PDF report — we'll send export instructions if you need them), scanned electrical drawings (redlined sets welcome), HMI screenshots, machine photos, and the 10-minute intake questionnaire.

STEP 2 — WE BUILD

Rung by rung, point by point

We parse the program, extract every I/O point and tag, map the logic routine by routine, and cross-reference your drawings and photos. Anything the files can't prove is flagged as a question for your staff — never guessed.

STEP 3 — YOU RECEIVE

The complete package

A six-part document, delivered as PDF in 2–3 weeks, checked against a mandatory quality checklist before it ships: every point, every routine, every alarm accounted for — with the counts to prove it.

The deliverable

Six documents your crew will actually use

1 · Sequence of Operations

What the machine does, step by step, in plain English — readable by anyone on the floor, not just the controls people.

2 · Logic Narrative

Every routine in the program explained — interlocks, timers, tracking, and the undocumented quirks your senior electrician carries in his head.

3 · Complete I/O List

Every point — wired and spare — described and cross-referenced to your drawings, terminals, and network devices.

4 · Alarm List

Every alarm in the program, what actually causes it, and what the operator should do about it — including the misleading ones.

5 · Troubleshooting Guide

The ten most likely failure modes for your machine — symptom, causes in order, and diagnostic steps an electrician can follow at 2 a.m.

6 · Network Diagram

What talks to what, every address, and what breaks when each link goes down. For many plants, the map that never existed.

Questions we get

FAQ

We don't know how to export our PLC program. Is that a problem?

No. Tell us your platform on the intake form and we'll send step-by-step export instructions for your software — usually a 10-minute job for whoever maintains the PLC. Best practice is a fresh upload from the running processor, and we'll walk you through that too.

Our drawings are ancient and covered in pen marks. Should we even send them?

Yes — especially those. The redlined set is the truthful set. Phone photos of paper prints are completely fine.

How do you handle confidentiality?

Your program files and drawings are used only to produce your package, are never shared or reused, and are deleted on request after delivery. We're happy to sign your NDA.

What platforms do you cover?

Allen-Bradley Logix (CompactLogix/ControlLogix) and legacy (SLC-500, MicroLogix, PLC-5), Siemens (TIA Portal, STEP 7), and most others — Automation Direct, Mitsubishi, Omron, GE. If you're unsure, ask; we'll tell you straight whether it's a fit.

What if the program doesn't match what's actually running?

The intake asks exactly that question. If you're not sure, we'll flag it and recommend a fresh upload from the processor first — documenting the wrong file helps nobody, and we'd rather wait a day for the right one.

Can you fix the problems you find in our logic?

The package will name them precisely (the sample found a missing drive fault-reset and an alarm pointing at the wrong message). Small corrective recommendations are included in writing; implementation is your team's or your integrator's call — we'll hand them everything they need.
